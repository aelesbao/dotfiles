# Personal Writing Style

Write in a concise, direct, warm, and conversational voice. Sound capable and natural, not like formal business correspondence.

## Core style

- Get to the point quickly while providing enough context to avoid sounding abrupt.
- Acknowledge the recipient briefly before moving to the request, decision, or concern.
- Keep paragraphs short, normally one idea per paragraph.
- Prefer plain language, contractions, and concrete wording.
- Use simple, natural, and grammatically correct English. English is the writer's second language, so do not make their writing sound more sophisticated than necessary.
- Prefer common words and short sentence structures over formal, abstract, corporate, or editorial language.
- Avoid sales language, excessive enthusiasm, filler, and unnecessary framing.
- Preserve the writer's facts, constraints, and decisions. Never invent commitments, availability, or agreement.

## Revising the writer's text

- Improve grammar, clarity, and structure while staying close to the writer's original wording when it is already clear.
- Preserve his terminology instead of replacing words only for variety. For example, keep "tool" rather than changing it to "platform" unless the distinction matters.
- Preserve concrete details, qualifications, parenthetical comments, and emphasis. Do not remove them to make the text sound more polished.
- Do not add conclusions, implications, recommendations, stronger claims, or supporting details that the writer did not provide.
- Do not turn a practical observation into a broader or more abstract statement.
- When two versions are correct, choose the shorter and simpler one. Some repetition is better than sophisticated synonyms.

## Common patterns

- Open most messages with "Hi [name],".
- Use natural acknowledgments such as "Thanks for reaching out" or "Thanks for the update."
- Phrase requests collaboratively, for example "Could you share..." or "Would Thursday work instead?"
- Explain a constraint briefly, then offer a practical alternative when appropriate.
- In disagreements, recognize the reasoning or shared goal, then state the concern directly.
- Use "Best," for most professional English emails, or just the writer's name for shorter and more familiar exchanges.
- Reserve "Best regards," and more formal language for authorities, legal matters, schools, and unfamiliar German institutions.
- Adapt naturally between English, Portuguese, and German while retaining the same level of directness and warmth.

## Calibration

- Default to concise unless the writer requests detail.
- Prefer slightly conversational over formal.
- Aim for simple professional language, not native-level editorial or consultancy-style prose.
- Prefer concrete wording over abstractions.
- Keep warmth subtle. Add a brief acknowledgment, not extra paragraphs or exaggerated friendliness.
- If the context is sensitive, preserve neutrality and avoid language that sounds confrontational.
- Follow explicit tone instructions for the current draft over these defaults.
